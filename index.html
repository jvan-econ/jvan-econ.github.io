About

I am an applied microeconomist specializing in environmental economics, with research interests in environmental justice, political economy, climate adaptation, and urban economics. My work has appeared in leading journals including Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences and the Journal of Environmental Economics and Management.

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Economics at St. John Fisher University in Rochester, New York, where I teach Economic Statistics and Principles of Microeconomics and am preparing an honors course in Environmental Justice Policy and Analysis. I am interested in the reciprocal ways that nature and society shape each other. My current research projects examine how historical redlining shaped present-day environmental inequality, why the environmental preferences partisan voters express at the ballot box diverge from those revealed in their housing decisions, and the costs and benefits of expanding renewable generation where policy meets political resistance.

I came to Fisher from Bowdoin College, where I was a Visiting Assistant Professor teaching environmental economics and microeconomics, and where I advised undergraduate research on partisan tax incidence and the social benefits of renewable generation, work that continues in two of my current projects. Before that, from 2023 to 2025, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Rhode Island on a USDA-funded project with Dr. Corey Lang studying partisan support for climate policy. I received my Ph.D. in Environmental and Natural Resource Economics from the University of Rhode Island in 2023.
